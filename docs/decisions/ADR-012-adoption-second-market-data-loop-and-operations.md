# ADR-012: Adoption: a second market, the data loop, pilot evaluation and operations

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Decider: engineering, building Phase 7 on the criteria below (delegated design decisions, as for
  ADR-006, ADR-009, ADR-010 and ADR-011); for Tsang Cheuk Nam's review. Three rows are flagged
  for confirmation: the second market (Taiwan), the pilot's wait-list control group (R59, new)
  and front-end hosting on AWS (closes ADR-001's open item).

## Context

Phase 7 (docs/08-roadmap.md) makes the method adoptable: the data loop and partner insights, a
second market by swapping data feeds, the pilot's evaluation, and the deployment, with the
PostgreSQL job queue for batch jobs over the book and a load test of the API. Left open by
earlier phases: the job queue (ADR-011), hosting for the front ends (ADR-001), the pilot gates
(`docs/07-pilot-and-metrics.md`), the `pilot_evaluation` and `partner_value` models, and a load
test against PostgreSQL (`docs/performance.md`).

Building a second market is the test of ADOPTION.md's claim that "another market swaps the source
files and adapters and keeps its catalogue". It found what was still Hong Kong in the code: the
route list read from one named source (`mtr_line_status`), adapters chosen by source id, a
currency symbol and allocation step written into the apps, and no way to match a trigger
reported per county or district to a customer (the territory-wide Hong Kong signals never
needed it; Hong Kong's district-zoned event types, air quality and road closures, could not pay
anyone either).

Criteria, in order: a market is config and data, never a fork of the code; a trigger is an
official, structured, published announcement; rates change only through the actuary, never by
the system learning on its own (R31); batch work over the book is idempotent and survives a
crash, as payouts are (R27); gates are fixed before the pilot starts; the mechanisms read like
existing practice, so the insurer, its auditors and the regulator recognise them.

## Decision

| Question | Decision | Industry basis |
|---|---|---|
| The second market (for confirmation) | **Taiwan.** It shares the typhoon peril with Hong Kong, so the weather income cover carries over as a method, but its official signals, regulator (Financial Supervisory Commission), privacy law (Personal Data Protection Act), currency (TWD), wording and data sources all differ, and its warnings are per county. Microinsurance (微型保險) is an established product line there | A local product built on a group product framework: the same product design, local filing, local triggers and wording |
| How a market is configured | A market overlay: `config/markets/<market>/` (selected by `MARKET`), read over `config/`. A file in the overlay replaces the base file of the same name; `catalogue/`, `languages/` and `copy/` merge by file name (a market replaces a cover's file, or adds one); `triggers/` is replaced as a whole when the overlay has one, since data sources never cross a border; `event_types.yaml` merges by id. The overlay holds its insurer profile as `insurer.yaml`. `just check` loads every market | One codebase with country configuration; local schedules over a regional product framework |
| Event types across markets | An event type id names one peril everywhere (`weather.tropical_cyclone`); its stages are the market's own official scale (Hong Kong signals 1 to 10; Taiwan's sea and land warnings). A market redefines an id's stages in its overlay; the ids it never redefines stay as in the base registry | Index triggers defined on the local authority's official scale |
| Taiwan's income trigger | `work.suspension`: the local government's announcement that work is suspended (停止上班), per county or city, published by the Directorate-General of Personnel Administration (DGPA) and announced by each local government under the rules for natural-disaster work suspension. It is the official, structured fact that work stops, as Hong Kong's Signal 8 and black rainstorm are under the Labour Department's code. Typhoon warnings (Central Weather Administration) stop new weather cover (R06); they do not pay | A parametric trigger on the official declaration, not a model output; the authority's own definition of when work stops |
| Adapters | A generic **CAP 1.2** adapter (Common Alerting Protocol, OASIS; ITU-T X.1303): alerts with `msgType`, `eventCode`, `onset`, `expires` and `area/geocode`. Taiwan's CWA warnings and DGPA work suspensions are both published in CAP through the national alert platform (NCDR), so one adapter serves both; many national services publish CAP, so most markets add a source file, not code. Each `config/triggers/<id>.yaml` names its `adapter` kind; the workers build every source from config, never from a list of ids. A source maps the feed's codes to event types and stages (R55) and, for zoned feeds, the feed's geocodes to the market's registered `areas` | The international standard for public warnings; adapters per data format, not per market |
| Declared work area (R55 amended) | A cover may match a zoned trigger on the customer's declared work areas (`match: declared_area`), as it matches a declared route or ticket: a registered area (`areas` of a data source), declared by the customer, copied into the policy for each period. A cover matched on area has no exposure without one (R42). Areas count toward accumulation per area (R51). The work area is where the insured work is done, never an AI input (R19); it is rated only if registered as a rating factor (R33) | The insured location of a parametric cover; accumulation by zone |
| One typhoon, many counties (R51 amended) | A peril may be counted `across_zones`: its trigger events in every zone form one loss occurrence with one event limit, as the reinsurance event definition counts one typhoon however many counties suspend work, where per-zone occurrences would have given each county its own limit. Everyone who declared an area counts toward the occurrence's benefit in force, so the guaranteed share is set prudently when the first county announces; each customer is owed only for the events in the areas they declared. An event announced for later (a suspension for tomorrow) is in force from its onset, never from the announcement, so an evening worker is not paid for the evening it was announced | The hours clause applied to the physical event across the territory |
| Money on screen | The apps format money from the market's ISO 4217 code and the customer's language with the platform's `Intl.NumberFormat`, and take the allocation step from the registry (`GET /registry`); neither is written into code | Locale-aware currency display |
| The job queue | One PostgreSQL table, `jobs`: a job has a kind, a unique key, a payload, a due time and a lease. A worker claims due jobs with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, so any number of workers share the work without a broker; a job whose lease runs out (a crashed worker) is claimed again; a failed job retries with exponential backoff and after `max_attempts` stays failed for a person to see. Enqueueing names a key (`open_policy_period/<policy>/<period>`): a key is enqueued once, so a schedule that runs twice, or two workers scheduling at once, add one job. Every job is also idempotent in what it writes (versioned store writes, ledger keys, R27). An in-memory queue passes the same tests | A transactional outbox and work queue in the system of record; at-least-once delivery with idempotent handlers |
| The batch jobs | Daily, in market local time, a fan-out job enqueues one job per policy due: `ProposeRenewal` (`renewal_notice_days` before a period: the re-rated default is issued and kept as issued, R35), `OpenPolicyPeriod` (on the period's first day: the customer's endorsement or the issued default becomes the schedule, the premium is billed, R03, R04, R34; a cancelled customer's policy is not renewed, a lapsed one is not opened, R41, R53), `EraseCancelled` (after cancellation, the optional data under R20 is erased and an erasure record kept, R54) and, monthly, `ExperienceStudy`. Points fade as a function of the date they are read on (R12), so no job rewrites them; payouts are sampled for audit when recorded (R14) and the fairness report is computed with the queue (R19) | Batch renewal processing with renewal notices issued and kept; data-subject erasure with a record of completion |
| The data loop | `learning::experience` turns the store into an experience study: per class of business and policy period, the exposure (policy periods, sum insured in force), claims and payouts (count and amount), actual against expected (A/E) from the rating basis in force, and a limited-fluctuation credibility weight; per prevention measure, claim frequency among customers with a verified activity against those without. It is aggregate, carries no customer id, and is an indication for the actuary: `python/economics` (`experience`) turns it into a proposed rating basis and prevention basis that take effect only when the actuary files them (R18, R31, R43) | Experience studies (actual vs expected) with limited-fluctuation credibility; rate changes filed by the appointed actuary |
| Partner insights (R21) | Partner results gain the funnel and retention: quotes shown, customers bound (conversion), policies renewed past their first period, rewards granted and redeemed (redemption rate), each withheld below `partner_insights_min_cohort` | Distribution MI with small-cell suppression |
| Pilot gates | `config/pilot.yaml`: the pilot's dates, the date the gates were fixed (before the start, checked at load) and each gate: a metric, a bound and its kind. A `stop` gate breached stops the pilot; scaling needs every `scale` gate passed and measured; anything else holds. Metrics come from the store (`learning::pilot`, `GET /ops/pilot`) and from outside it (satisfaction surveys, the partner's revenue), passed in; a metric not measured never passes a gate. `microinsurance_economics.pilot_evaluation` evaluates the report | Stage gates with pre-registered success and stopping criteria |
| The control group (R59, new; for confirmation) | A wait-list control: a share `pilot.control.share` of customers bound during the pilot, drawn from the customer id and the insurer's recorded seed, is offered no prevention tasks for their first `pilot.control.periods` policy periods, then joins. Everything else is the same, and the key features say so. Claim frequency on loss-based covers is compared between the groups: the claims avoided against control | Randomised controlled pilot with a wait-list control, so no one is denied the benefit for long |
| Deployment (front-end hosting for confirmation) | Terraform for AWS (`infra/terraform`): one deployment per market, in a region in or near the market for data residency (Hong Kong `ap-east-1`, Taipei `ap-east-2`), with its own KMS key; a VPC whose database subnets have no route out; RDS PostgreSQL 16, Multi-AZ, encrypted, TLS only, 35 days of point-in-time recovery and monthly copies kept for `record_retention_years` in a locked AWS Backup vault (R54); ECS Fargate services for the API (behind an ALB reachable only from CloudFront) and the workers, from one image; an S3 bucket for evidence (KMS, private, versioned, TLS only); secrets (`API_TOKEN_SECRET`, `AUDIT_SEED`, `DATABASE_URL`, the model key) created empty and filled out of band, so none is in Terraform state; alarms on errors, p99 latency, healthy tasks and the database. The apps are served by the API service on the same origin, behind CloudFront with AWS WAF: only their static files are cached, no API response rests at the edge, and a customer's token never crosses an origin. This closes ADR-001's open hosting item: AWS, not Cloudflare | Standard insurer cloud deployment: data residency, encryption at rest, managed database, secrets management, WORM backups for record retention |
| Accumulation check at scale (found by the load test) | Every quote measured benefit in force across the whole book to apply the zone accumulation limit (R51), so a quote's cost grew with the book. Now a job measures it every `accumulation.refresh_minutes` (default 5) and keeps the result; a quote reads that one record, and measures afresh only when the record is older than twice the interval. New allocation is thus checked against a measure at most minutes old, which the limit's margin absorbs | Accumulation managed on a periodic exposure snapshot, as reinsurance accumulation reports are |
| Notices from the policy store (found by the load test) | Each write was a three-statement transaction with `pg_notify`, and NOTIFY serialises the commits that send one. A write is now one statement; only collections streamed to customers (payouts) send a notice, in the same statement | Event notification only where something listens |
| Load test | `cargo run --release -p microinsurance-api --example load_test` binds new customers through a partner's embedded journey (token exchange, declarations, quote, the tap), then drives a weighted mix of the non-AI routes customers, the partner and the insurer's people read, over HTTP against a running API, and reports p50, p95 and p99 per route against `docs/performance.md`. Its results and what is still open are recorded there | Performance testing against stated non-functional requirements before go-live |

## Consequences

- R51 is amended (occurrences across zones; the accumulation snapshot), R55 (declared work areas, a market's own stages, in force from onset); R59 is new; R54's erasure is a recorded job.
- New: the market overlay (`crates/config`), `config/markets/tw/`, the CAP adapter
  (`crates/triggers/src/adapters/cap.rs`), `MatchKey::DeclaredArea` and `work_areas` on the profile
  and policy, `crates/workers/src/queue.rs` and `jobs.rs`, `learning::experience` and
  `learning::pilot`, `config/pilot.yaml`, `python/economics` `experience`, `pilot_evaluation`,
  `partner_value`, the Terraform modules and the load test.
- `declared_routes` reads every source's `lines`; the workers build sources from each file's
  `adapter`; the apps and consoles no longer assume Hong Kong dollars or the allocation step; the store lists several customers in one query; the API's PostgreSQL pool is `DATABASE_POOL_SIZE`; a worker with `WORKER_POLLS=0` runs jobs only. The infra Dockerfile now lays the image out as the code reads it (`REPO_ROOT`, the prompts, the built apps).
- Open: the real DGPA and CWA CAP feed URLs and codes (the fixtures are synthetic, as Hong Kong's
  history export is); Taiwan's filed rates; the pilot's gate values with the insurer. A work-area rating
  factor (counties differ in how often they suspend work) with the actuary. The partner results
  and the ops queue read the whole book per request, and binding takes five writes: see
  `docs/performance.md`. `terraform validate` against the AWS provider has not been run here.
